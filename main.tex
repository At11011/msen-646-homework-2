\begin{enumerate}
  \item The electrode potential of titanium immersed in a sulfuric
    acid solution of pH approximately 1 was
    measured to be \SI{-0.70}{\volt} vs. SCE
    \begin{enumerate}[(a)]
      \item Confirm that titanium undergoes active corrosion under these
        conditions and
      \item consult the Pourbaix diagram to suggest three
        different means to provide corrosion protection.
    \end{enumerate}
  \item If iron is immersed in a solution of pH 3.0, what electrode potential
    must be maintained to prevent the evolution of hydrogen gas, and what
    conditions (describe in detail)?
  \item Use the following data for the corrosion of iron in hydrogen-saturdated,
    oxygen-free solution, calculate:

    \begin{enumerate}[(a)]
      \item the corrosion rate and
      \item the exchange current density for the hydrogen evolution reaction
        (HER) on iron: \par
    \end{enumerate}
    pH = 4, $\alpha_{\ch{Fe^{2+}}} = \SI{0.02}{M}, i_{\ch{Fe}}^O
    = \SI{9e-7}{\ampere\per\centi\meter\squared}$; anodic
    $d\eta / d(\log i) = \SI{0.02}{\volt\per decade}$; the corrosion
    potential of iron is \SI{-0.215}{\volt} vs RHE and
    $e_{\ch{Fe^{2+}}|\ch{Fe}} = \SI{-0.44}{\volt}$ vs. SHE. For the HER
    , the cathodic $d\eta / d(\log i) = \SI{-0.12}{\volt\per decade}$.
  \item Iron corrodes in a solution saturated with oxygen. The pH of
    the solution
    is 7 and the partial pressure of oxygen is \SI{1}{atm}.
    \begin{enumerate}[(a)]
      \item Calculate the corrosion current and corrosion potential of the
        system.
      \item Calculate the protection current required to reduce the corrosion
        current to zero.
    \end{enumerate}
    Additional information:

    \begin{align*}
      [\ch{Fe}] &= \SI{0.7}{m} &\mathrm{pH} &= 7 &P_{\ch{O_2}} &= \SI{1}{atm} \\
      b_a &= \SI{0.08}{\volt\per decade} & b_c &=
      \SI{-0.11}{\volt\per decade} \\
      i_{\ch{Fe}}^O &= \SI{e-5}{\ampere\per\centi\meter\squared} &
      i_{\ch{OH}}^O &= \SI{e-6}{\ampere\per\centi\meter\squared}
    \end{align*}

  \item Calculate the corrosion potential, corrosion current, and protection
    current needed to stop corrosion for cadmium in a corrosive
    deaerated medium.

    Additional information:

    \begin{align*}
      [\ch{Cd^{2+}}] = \SI{e-5}{M} & pH &=  1 & a_c &=
      \SI{-0.360}{\volt} vs. SHE \\
      b_a &= \SI{0.100}{\volt\per decade} & b_c &=
      \SI{-0.125}{\volt\per decade} & i_{\ch{Cd}}^O &=
      \SI{e-3}{\ampere\per\centi\meter\squared} \\
    \end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
